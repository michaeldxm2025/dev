Company overview

TEPCO is Japan's largest utility and one of the world's largest by capacity and revenue. Following the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster in March 2011, TEPCO underwent a profound restructuring under government intervention, with the state acquiring a majority stake through the Nuclear Damage Compensation and Decommissioning Facilitation Corporation (NDF).

The company is organised into three pillars: Fuel & Power (wholesale generation and trading), TEPCO Power Grid (regulated transmission and distribution for Kanto), and Renewable Power (renewables development and investment). The Fukushima decommissioning programme remains a multi-decade commitment, with estimated costs exceeding ¥20 trillion.

Strategy and transition

TEPCO's Zero Carbon 2050 Vision targets carbon neutrality. Near-term priorities include offshore wind development (targeting multi-GW in Tohoku and Kanto offshore zones), battery storage deployment for grid stabilisation, and hydrogen/ammonia co-firing at thermal plants as transitional decarbonisation.

The company has entered a joint venture with Chubu Electric Power (JERA) for thermal power operations and LNG procurement, which is now one of Japan's largest power generators. TEPCO and Tohoku Electric have also formed Kanto-Tohoku Power Grid for transmission operations.
